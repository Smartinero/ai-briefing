Idea 1 · Keywords

Help authors find keywords, not just manage them

How Amazon picks which ad to show

your bid + likelihood of a sale

Amazon earns on the click and the sale, so relevance and conversion count for more than the bid alone. (Two campaign types: product targeting and keyword bidding.)

My working theory, worth testing: only exact-match keywords build organic rank for that keyword; broad, phrase and auto don't. If true, move winners to exact quickly (see idea 4). Auto campaigns are fine for a total beginner to see Amazon's picks, just not the best approach.

What does seem to hold: Kindlepreneur's "Popularity Effect" experiment (Dave Chesson) found steady sales from any source, Amazon Ads included, get a book shown for more keywords and ranked higher. Amazon starts with the narrow terms where it converts, then tests broader searches. He never split sales by match type, so exact-only stays my theory.

1

Mine successful competitors

Look through their product descriptions and A+ content for terms that keep coming up.

2

"Book on procrastination" → semantically related words readers might use.

Keywords nobody clicks cost nothing, so going wide is cheap.

On the exact-match theory: Amazon publishes no rule that match type (or ad sales at all) affects organic rank. Sales do feed ranking signals, and practitioners like exact match because it concentrates them on one term, but the one small published split test found little effect. So: unproven. zbdgrowth.com · landingcube.com · Popularity Effect: kindlepreneur.com · Bid + relevance: Amazon Ads FAQ ("we don't share the details of relevance").
Idea 2 · Impression share

Before raising a bid, check whether it can actually help

The KPI

Top-of-search / first-page impression share: how often you show up on page 1 when someone searches that keyword.

e.g. ~80% = you're on page 1 for most searches

Why it's a differentiator: almost nobody uses impression share and bid vs. CPC together. Most tools only look at ACoS.

Why page 1 matters: an often-quoted study found ~70% of Amazon shoppers don't click past the first results page. Good enough as a direction, but the data is old (see footnote).

The "70%" comes from a Millward Brown / Compete analysis of 75 million Amazon searchers in Sept 2014: only 30% clicked past page 1, and the first result got ~35% of clicks. It's about clicks and page views, not sales, it's 12 years old, and it's often wrongly called "Amazon's own data". Adweek · MarketingProfs
Idea 3 · Zombie keywords

Wake the zombies up once, or let them go

What a zombie is

A keyword with 0 or just 1–3 impressions, so it never gets a click. I see them even on broad and phrase, which I find suspicious.

Two likely causes

Amazon thinks it's irrelevant, or nobody actually searches for it. Amazon's selection logic is opaque and changes often.

Why bother?

They cost nothing, but some may be hiding potential just because the bid is too low.

Step 1

Relevance check

Does it make sense at all? E.g. an LLM check, or match it against the listing.

→
Step 2

Double the bid

If it's plausible, double the bid for a short test window.

Step 3

Watch the funnel

Do impressions turn into clicks, and clicks into sales?

Yes → keep it

A live keyword again.

No → retire it

Clear it out as ballast.

Idea 4 · Broad → exact ★ my favourite

A keyword loop that gets smarter every round

1

Broad seed

e.g. procrastination. Matches any search that contains it.

2

Search term report

Shows what customers actually typed. Find the terms that convert.

3

Add as EXACT

book about procrastination goes into the exact campaign.

4

Negative in broad

Same term as negative exact in the broad campaign, so the two don't compete.

RepeatEach round adds proven exact keywords and a cleaner broad campaign

Why it's perfect for your tool

By hand this is brutal: tables, cross-checking, testing. For software it's an ideal job. It's been my dream feature for years, and I think it's the strongest pitch for your app.

Why it matters for authors

The goal is organic sales, not just paid ones. If my exact-match theory holds, this loop is how ranking gets built. Beginner advice says "just run auto", but that may not build rank. Worth testing.

Bonus: your tool could settle it

Push some keywords with exact-match ads and comparable ones with broad, then compare how their organic rank moves. Nobody has published this test properly, so your tool could be the one to answer it.

Happy to keep helping as this grows. — Sven
