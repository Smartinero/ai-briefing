3 · Everyday polish: drafts, approvals and routines

Two app releases in the window (0.68.0 on Oct 5, 0.68.1 on Oct 7). Email drafts can go out from a verified Gmail "send-as" address. Approval cards now name the Bot and quote its instruction. 📘 Grok Bot docs (official how-to) docs.x.ai/grok-bot
Tag @bot on X
DocsHigh relevance updated Oct 7 · ~4 min read
How to hand any X post to your Grok Bot: reply with, for example, "@bot summarize this thread and send the key points to my Slack". @bot posts only a short confirmation on X, and the work happens in your main Bot's chat. You need a Grok account with your X account connected (grok.com → Settings → Account → Connect), Grok Bot access, and at least one Bot of your own. It is not yet available for X accounts based in Australia, the UK, the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein or Norway, and availability goes by the country X has on record, not your location. @bot stays quiet if the post also tags @grok or contains a video or GIF.
Whether tagging works for your account depends on the country X has on record for it, which I could not check.
Skills and routines
DocsHigh relevance updated Oct 6 · ~6 min read
The official method: "Start with a one-time task. Make it reliable, save the method as a skill, and only then automate it." A good skill states when to use it, the inputs and access it needs, the steps, how to check the result, what to return, and what needs approval. "Teach a task" lets you demonstrate a browser workflow (up to ten minutes, no microphone audio). For a routine, confirm the owning Bot, the schedule and time zone, the input, the expected result, the approval boundary, and what happens when a source is missing. Use Test run before enabling. A Bot can own up to 50 routines.
This page says to type "/" to reference a saved skill, but the Oct 7 changelog says "/" no longer opens that menu. One of the two is out of date.
