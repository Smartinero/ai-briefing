🎯 You asked for: adapting prompting to Opus 5

Scope note The two authoritative sources on this are Anthropic's own, and both were published July 24, 2026 — outside this week's window. They are included because you asked for them, not because they are news. What is in-window is that two of your sources (Enovair, and the wildcard) built on them this week.

1 · Prompting Claude Opus 5 — Anthropic docs · ~8 min read (estimated). The guide's framing is that Opus 5's default behaviour changed, so prompts written for earlier models now over-correct. Concretely, it says Opus 5 writes longer by default, narrates more during agentic work, self-verifies without being asked, expands scope beyond what was requested, and delegates to subagents more readily. Its remedies are explicit counter-instructions:

  • Length: "Keep responses focused, brief, and concise… spend most of the response on the main answer."
  • Scope: "Deliver what was asked, at the scope intended. If the request seems mistaken, say so in a sentence and continue with the task as asked."
  • Deliverables: "Match the length of written documents to what the task needs… do not pad with filler sections, redundant summaries, or boilerplate."
  • Self-correction: only correct an earlier statement when the error would change your conclusions or decisions.

And — this is the part that matters most for you — it says to remove instructions that earlier models needed: explicit "include a final verification step" instructions now cause over-verification, and "double-check your answer" adds cost without improving results. It also recommends using lower effort levels (low, medium) rather than disabling thinking.

2 · The new rules of context engineering for Claude 5 generation models — Anthropic blog, by Thariq Shihipar · ~6 min read (estimated). Six then→now shifts: rules → judgment; examples → interface design; upfront information → progressive disclosure (move detail into skills Claude calls when needed); repetition → concise descriptions placed where they are relevant; manual memory → auto-memory; simple specs → rich references. The headline evidence is the 80% system-prompt reduction in Claude Code with no measured performance loss.

Open question There is a real tension between this guidance and your own operating framework, and it is worth resolving deliberately rather than by drift. Your structured methodology deliberately instructs verification (Watson) and challenge (Frank) — the docs above say verification instructions specifically are now counter-productive on Opus 5. These are not the same thing, and the distinction is the whole question. Anthropic is talking about redundant self-checking of its own output; Watson is a source-traceability standard the model would not apply on its own. Hypothesis: the Watson standard survives and the "add a final verification step" mechanics do not. That is testable — run one substantial task with your methodology skill as-is and one with the verification mechanics stripped but the source-labelling standard kept, and compare. No source claims this; it is a proposal, not a finding.
