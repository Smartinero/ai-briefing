3
What a Bot remembers, and how to check it
📘 Docs, Create and manage Bots · Nov 5 · also Matt Palmer (Nov 9 clip)
Each Bot has its own memory, but a few facts about you, such as your name and time zone, are shared by all your Bots. A Bot decides on its own what to save, and "doesn't ask first." There is no memory screen for a personal Bot, so you check it in chat: "What do you remember about me?" To fix something: "That's out of date." To remove it: "Forget that I'm on the launch team." Resetting a conversation keeps memory. Deleting a Bot deletes it. The docs add that memory "is not a source of truth": ask the Bot to look up prices and other changing numbers again. Matt Palmer's 90-second clip explains the same split between memory about you and each Bot's own memory.

What to try next (assessment)

TryCheck your Bot's memory in two minutes. Open your primary Bot and send: "What do you remember about me? List the facts all my Bots share separately from what only you know." Correct anything wrong in plain words ("That's out of date: …"). This is read-only until you correct something.

TryFind out what uses your allowance. Ask: "Break down my usage this week by Bot, routine and group chat, and tell me which routine costs the most. Don't change anything." Then decide for yourself whether a routine can run less often, for example daily instead of hourly.

TrySet one Ask first rule for anything you can't undo. The new docs page says: Settings → General, under Bot, open Custom Rules. Describe the action in plain words, for example "send an email", and choose Ask first. (Edition 3 described this list under Settings → Permissions. Use whichever your app version shows.)

TryGive a Bot its job in one message. In a new Bot's first chat, send something like: "Your job: keep track of my home and car maintenance. Sources: my calendar and email. Never book or pay for anything without asking me. Remember this." The Bots page says to give a new Bot "its job, sources, and approval boundaries" in the first message and ask it to remember them.

WatchPhone calls. Wait for an official docs page before you rely on it. Creators report US numbers only, which would rule out businesses in Thailand.

📘 Grok Bot docs (official how-to) docs.x.ai/grok-bot
How Grok Bot Works
DocsNewHigh relevance updated Nov 6 · ~10 min read
New in the docs sitemap this week. It follows one request through five steps. 1. Memory fills in what you didn't say: last year's pass, and your rule to use the work card. 2. A connected app (Gmail) finds the receipt, which the page calls "more reliable than clicking through pages in a browser." 3. The computer signs in, looking for a login in this order: Vault, then 1Password, then a Secure Form. For a passkey or CAPTCHA, the Bot hands you the computer: Open computer → Take control, then tell it to continue. 4. An Ask first rule stops the email to finance. 5. It logs the expense in Drive and reports back, even if your laptop is closed. The safety notes: "A Bot has no account or credentials of its own," so it can't do anything you couldn't. "Your Bots aren't separated from each other." Sign out of services a Bot no longer needs, and "Treat anything on the computer as available to all your Bots."
Payment line, unchanged from edition 3: "A saved card fills in at checkout without asking you each time … If you'd rather approve every payment, don't save the card." Matt Palmer's Nov 2 roundup says "You approve every purchase." Not resolved.
Create and manage Bots
DocsHigh relevance updated Nov 5 · ~9 min read
Creating a Bot is now New Bot (the + at the top of the sidebar) or Cmd/Ctrl+N, then a name, shape and colour. "Personal Bots no longer have a description or a Bot settings page; to change what a Bot does, tell it in chat." A new section, What a Bot remembers, explains memory (see highlight 3). It's shared across all your chats with that Bot. In a group chat, "everyone in the group can read what your Bot says," so tell your Bot if something should stay private. Useful extras: on iPhone, Duplicate copies a Bot's settings, skills and routines but not its memory. Hide from sidebar keeps a Bot and its routines running, while Delete erases its memory and can't be undone. Sharing a Bot as a template creates a public link that exposes "its identity, description, skills, and routines", so remove anything private first.
